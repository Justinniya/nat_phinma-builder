# Pitch: Rent Ledger for Mama Lita

Three minutes, spoken. If more than one of you presents, write a name
next to each beat.

## The script

### 1. The person (~45s) — Presenter: ______

I live in a boarding house. The owner is Mama Lita. She has about ten
rooms, and every month I watch the same scene.

She sits at her table with a notebook. Her phone is open to Messenger.
She is flipping pages, scrolling through chats, and counting cash. She
is trying to answer one question: who has paid?

Some of us paid her in cash. Some paid her helper. A few sent GCash.
And sometimes she has to come to a boarder and ask, "Did you really
pay?" It is awkward for her, and it is awkward for us.

### 2. Today (~30s) — Presenter: ______

Today, everything lives in a paper notebook. Most of us pay face to
face, so there is often no record except her handwriting. If she is
busy with chores, she writes it down later, or she forgets. If the
helper took the cash, she has to remember to ask.

It costs her hours every month, and it costs trust between her and her
boarders.

### 3. Our fix (~45s) — Presenter: ______

We are building a simple rent ledger on her phone. Boarders report
their payments, and Mama Lita confirms them.

When I pay, I open the app and tap "I paid." I choose cash, and who I
handed it to. Mama Lita sees it on her list. When the money is in her
hand, she taps Confirm, and my room turns green.

On one screen, she sees every room: paid, due soon, or overdue. She
can also record deposits and payments herself. No formulas. No
scrolling through chats. Big buttons.

### 4. The demo moment (~30s) — Presenter: ______

In Session 3, we will use two phones. On one, a boarder reports a cash
payment. On the other, Mama Lita confirms it, and the room turns green.
Then we will ask her, "Did Room 4 pay in August?" She will answer in
under ten seconds, without opening the notebook.

### 5. How we'll know (~30s) — Presenter: ______

Before Session 3, Mama Lita and at least three boarders will use the
app for a real payment cycle, alongside the notebook. We will bring
back two things. First, did boarders actually report their payments,
and did she confirm them? Second, can she find who has paid in under
ten seconds? We will tell you honestly what worked and what did not.

## Hard questions to prepare for

**1. "Has Mama Lita actually said she will use this?"**
This is our weakest point right now. Our evidence is real: one of us
lives there and has watched the struggle many times. But we haven't
formally asked her yet. Honest answer, if it is still true on the day:
"Not yet. We are asking her this week. If she says no, we will test it
with boarders using her notebook's data, and tell you that." Better:
ask her before the pitch, so you can say "Yes, she agreed."

**2. "Most people pay cash face to face. Why would a boarder bother
opening an app after handing over money?"**
Honest answer: "Because it protects them. Today the only proof of a
cash payment is Mama Lita's handwriting. A confirmed report in the app
is their receipt. That matters most when the cash went to her helper,
not to her. And if a boarder doesn't bother, Mama Lita can still record
the payment herself in a few taps." Check this with 2–3 boarders before
you present. Ask them, "Would you use this?" and report what they say.

**3. "Why not just use a notebook more carefully, or a Google Sheet?"**
Honest answer: "She has tried the notebook for years. It is the
problem. She never uses a laptop, and a spreadsheet on a phone is tiny
and easy to break. Boarders also can't safely write into her
spreadsheet. The app is just a ledger built for her phone, with one
job: who has paid?"
